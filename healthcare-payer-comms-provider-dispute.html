While serving as PR Manager for a large California health plan, I supported communications during a sensitive, high-profile legal dispute with an out-of-network behavioral health provider. The matter drew media attention because it involved behavioral health services, patient access, claims payment practices, and competing public narratives. The provider positioned itself as a smaller organization challenging a large insurer, creating a reputational risk that could quickly shape public perception if the organization remained silent.

The legal team initially preferred not to comment on coverage. That approach protected the litigation strategy, but it also risked allowing one side of the story to define the organization’s reputation. I advised senior leaders and legal counsel that silence was not a neutral choice. In an emotionally charged healthcare issue involving patient care, behavioral health services, and a perceived imbalance of power, a lack of response could be interpreted as indifference or unwillingness to address legitimate concerns.

My recommendation was to protect the legal strategy while still giving the organization a principled, values-based voice. The communications approach needed to respect the boundaries of active litigation while helping reporters and readers understand the broader public-interest context: member access, affordability, healthcare resources, and the responsibility to address suspected fraud, waste, and abuse.

We developed a response framework built around three priorities. First, we would protect the legal process by avoiding any discussion of the specific facts, allegations, or merits of active litigation. Second, we would reinforce the organization’s purpose by affirming its commitment to helping members access needed benefits and care. Third, we would explain why preventing fraud, waste, and abuse matters to Californians who rely on affordable healthcare coverage and stable benefits.

The resulting media statement was concise, disciplined, and grounded in public accountability:

Because this matter involves active legal proceedings, we cannot comment on the specific allegations or facts at issue. We remain committed to protecting our members' access to quality, affordable care and to addressing suspected fraud, waste, and abuse whenever it occurs. These practices can drive up healthcare costs and put benefits at risk for Californians who depend on them. We will continue to work with appropriate partners to protect the integrity of the healthcare system and the people it serves.

The statement did not attempt to win the legal argument in public. Instead, it gave reporters and readers the context silence would have withheld: the organization had a responsibility to protect members, safeguard healthcare resources, and preserve access to benefits. It also created a clear bridge between legal restraint and public accountability, allowing the organization to communicate responsibly while honoring the limits of an active case.

This experience reinforced several principles that continue to guide my approach to issues management. Silence can create its own narrative, especially when an issue involves vulnerable people and a perceived imbalance of power. Legal and communications teams have to work together, because effective issues management protects both the case and the organization’s long-term reputation. Values-based messaging is strongest when it is specific, credible, and tied to the public interest. And in healthcare, reputational response must always return to the people affected: members, families, providers, and communities.

By balancing legal discipline with a clear public-interest message, the response helped ensure the organization could speak responsibly during a sensitive and highly visible dispute.
